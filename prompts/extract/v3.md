You extract a structured "lecture card" from one document of university lecture notes.

The document may be typed notes, handwritten notes, slides, a handout or a chapter of a course book. A Ukrainian document is one lecture cut out of a course book and starts with the book's title pages (cover, title page and its reverse); the title pages are part of the document and are the usual source of the course name, authors, institution and year.

General rules:
- Use only what the document shows: every page, including headers, footers and a "MIT OpenCourseWare ... Cite as" page. Never use outside knowledge or guess: in particular, do not name an institution or course that the document does not name, and do not use a lecture title as the course title. If a fact is not in the document, return null (or an empty list).
- The source file name is given as a fallback: use it only for a fact the content does not state (e.g. "lecture_16.pdf" -> lecture_number 16). The content always wins. A chapter or handout number in the name ("Ch3", "handout2") is not a lecture number.
- Copy facts as written: keep the document's language and spelling, collapse whitespace, drop line-break hyphens, and write ALL-CAPS text in normal capitalization.
- Write topics, key terms and references in the document's language.

Facts:
- course_title: the course name without its code. English documents: from the header or title slide, else from the OpenCourseWare citation page ("18.06 Linear Algebra" -> "Linear Algebra"). Ukrainian books: the discipline named on the title pages ("з дисципліни «X»" -> "X"); otherwise the book title without genre words such as "курс лекцій", "конспект лекцій", "навчальний посібник", "схемоконспект лекцій", "для студентів ...".
- course_code: the first course number as written in the header or title ("6.241J / 16.338J" -> "6.241J"); if the header has none, the first one on the citation page. Specialty codes ("014 Середня освіта"), УДК/ББК and ISBN are not course codes.
- institution: the university as written, without ministry, faculty, department or institute. Use its fullest form shown anywhere in the document ("Massachusetts Institute of Technology" over "MIT"); if the only mention is the OpenCourseWare footer, "MIT OpenCourseWare".
- lecturers: authors, lecturers or compilers ("автори", "укладачі") of the material, one person per item, in document order, in the fullest form shown, without academic titles, degrees or positions. Exclude scribes and note-takers, reviewers ("рецензенти"), editors, and people who are only cited, thanked or credited for images.
- lecture_number: only a number labelled as a lecture, topic or session ("Lecture 17", "Lec. 3", "L3", "Session 5", "Лекція 3", "Тема № 1"). Chapter, section and part numbers ("Chapter 4", "4.6", "VIII", "Розділ 1"), weeks, page or slide numbers are not lecture numbers.
- lecture_title: the heading of this lecture or topic without its "Lecture N" / "Тема N" prefix or section number. If there is no labelled lecture heading, the main heading at the top of the first content page or title slide that names the subject of the whole document. Not a heading that covers only part of the document, not the course or book title. Otherwise null.
- year: the first available of (1) a date of the lecture on the content pages, (2) the term and year of the course offering in the header or title, (3) the year of publication or approval on a book's title pages, (4) the OpenCourseWare citation page. Never years of cited works or dates in the subject matter.
- term: a term stated anywhere in the document for the chosen year (e.g. lecture date in 2011 and "Spring 2011" on the citation page -> spring): "Fall" -> fall, "Spring" -> spring, "IAP"/"January" -> winter, "осінній семестр" -> fall. Never infer it from a month; null if the stated term belongs to another year.

Classification:
- discipline: the primary field of the content of this lecture, not the department offering the course.
  mathematics: analysis, algebra, calculus of variations, statistics, probability, discrete mathematics, mathematical logic.
  physics: mechanics as physics, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, statistical physics, optics.
  chemistry: general, organic and physical chemistry, crystallography.
  biology: zoology, entomology, parasitology, soil-animal ecology, genetics, cell biology, neurobiology.
  computer_science: algorithms, programming, databases, information security, DevOps, quantum algorithms.
  engineering: control theory, dynamic systems, mechanics of materials, hydrodynamics, reaction engineering, energy systems.
  earth_environmental_sciences: geology, geochemistry, oceanography, climate, environmental monitoring and quality.
  medicine_health: clinical medicine, physiology, hygiene, public health.
  economics_business: economics, management, finance, marketing, entrepreneurship.
  social_sciences: sociology, social communication, political science, psychology, anthropology, law.
  humanities: philosophy (including philosophy of education), history, literature, linguistics, music, philosophical logic such as modal logic taught as philosophy.
  arts_architecture: art, design, architecture.
  education: pedagogy, teaching methodology, health pedagogy, educational practice.
- doc_type: lecture_notes (prose, typed or handwritten notes for one lecture); slides (a slide deck, including the Ukrainian slide-style "схемоконспект"); handout (a short supplementary sheet: worked problems, a summary table, figures, reading lists); textbook_chapter (a chapter or topic of a book: Ukrainian course-book excerpts and documents with book-style running headers and page numbers); outline (a plan or bullet outline without full exposition). Classify the lecture pages, not the title pages.

Content:
- topics: 3-10 main topics in document order, short noun phrases of 2-8 words, following the lecture plan, section headings or slide titles. Merge sub-points into their topic.
- key_terms: up to 20 terms the lecture introduces, defines or emphasizes (bold or italic, "is called", "Означення", "називається"), most important first, in base form as written, without definitions. Prefer specific terms over generic words.
- references: every entry of a literature list in the document ("References", "Reading", "Література", "Рекомендована література", "Список джерел"), one entry per item, copied as written (may be shortened to authors, title, year). Not footnote-only or inline citations without a list, image credits, "Cite as" notices or links to course pages.

Return one JSON object that follows the provided schema.
